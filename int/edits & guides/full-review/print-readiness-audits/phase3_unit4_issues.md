## Steps 1–4 — Read, goal, activity map

### Step 1 — Focus statements

```text
By the end of this unit, I will be able to:
- identify the main parts of a professional email.
- write a short, clear subject line for a workplace email.
- write a simple professional email with an appropriate opening and closing.
```

Framing is consistent with the book standard. Three can-do statements. ✓

### Step 2 — Read all activities

Done before any editing. Full unit read from Focus to end.

### Step 3 — Teacher goal

Unit 4 teaches the six-part professional email structure. Students identify the parts, compare a weak and a strong example, practise subject lines and opening sentences in controlled exercises, write a complete guided email, then apply the structure independently in a freer task and a transfer activity.

### Steps 6–8 — Information order and instruction clarity

**Issue 20** — D: situation has no recipient  
"You want to confirm the required documents for an application" gives no recipient. The task asks for a `Greeting:` as a required output, so students need to know who they are writing to. Add a recipient relationship, e.g., "You are writing to an applicant, Ms. Kim."

**Issue 22** — E: contradictory output requirement and over-scaffolded Include list  
"write one complete email in **3-4 sentences**" is contradicted by the six-component Include list immediately below. In Units 1 and 2, sentence count and Include list are calibrated (3 items ≈ 3-4 sentences). Unit 4's Include list has five or six components, which is incompatible with 3-4 sentences. Fix: change to "Write one complete email." Remove the Include list entirely — E is freer practice and students should apply the structure from D without being re-scaffolded.

**Issue 24** — G: edit instruction undefined and missing output requirement  
"Rewrite the email so each part has a clear job" does not specify which parts, does not reference Activity A's six-part structure, and states no output requirement. Fix: name the six parts and state the expected output (e.g., "Rewrite the email using the six-part structure from Activity A. Write a complete email of 5-6 lines.").

**Issue 25** — H Transfer: pedagogical explanation in wrong position  
"This helps you separate the job of the subject line from the job of the opening sentence." appears after the three situations, as an afterthought. In Units 2 and 3, purpose statements come before task items. Move this sentence to the opening of the div.

### Step 9 — Editor-facing wording

**Issue 8** — C rewrite task: "SVO-style subjects" and "lower-level writers"

### Step 12 — List types

**Issue 21** — D: scaffold list should be numbered, not bulleted  
The six email components follow a required sequence (subject line → greeting → opening sentence → body → closing sentence → sign-off). Bullets signal unordered items; a numbered list correctly conveys that the order matters. No per-item placeholders are needed — the list is a guide reference and the PH-3 is the single response area for the complete email.

H Transfer numbered list (1. subject line, 2. opening sentence) is Case 2 — no change needed.
