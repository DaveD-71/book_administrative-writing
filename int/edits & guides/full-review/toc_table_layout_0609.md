# Administrative Writing, Intermediate

This textbook helps you build practical administrative writing skills step by step. You begin with the basics of clear sentences, professional tone, and paragraph structure. Then you move into common workplace writing tasks such as emails, clarification requests, delay updates, procedures, notices, summaries, and short explanations. In the later units, you combine these skills across multiple documents, revise earlier writing more carefully, and prepare a small portfolio of stronger final work.

The book is designed for intermediate-level learners who need English for real office communication. The goal is not to make your writing sound overly formal or complicated. The goal is to help you write messages that are clear, polite, well organised, and useful for the reader. Throughout the book, you will compare weaker and stronger examples, study useful language, practise writing with support, and then write more independently.

The book also teaches different kinds of workplace messages, not only external emails. You will practise internal messages for colleagues, short notices for teams, meeting summaries, problem explanations, procedures, clarification requests, correction messages, and sets of related documents written for different readers. Some tasks ask you to write to an outside office or applicant, while others ask you to explain the same situation inside your own team. This helps you understand how audience, purpose, tone, and level of detail change from one document type to another.

Each unit follows a clear learning sequence. First, you notice a writing problem or compare two versions. Next, you study the language, structure, or pattern that makes the stronger version work. After that, you practise the skill in guided and freer tasks, edit weak writing, and check your own progress. This repeated pattern helps you build control little by little instead of trying to write everything perfectly at once.

By the end of the book, you should be able to write short administrative texts with better control of accuracy, tone, clarity, and organisation. You should also be better able to adapt one situation for different readers, revise your own drafts, and choose language that helps the reader understand what happened, what is needed, and what should happen next.

## Module Overview

`Module 1` gives you the basic writing skills you need for the rest of the book. In these units, you learn how to write clear sentences, use a professional tone, and build a short paragraph that stays focused on one main idea. This module prepares you for longer and more detailed workplace messages.

`Module 2` focuses on short professional emails. You learn how to organise an email clearly, ask for clarification, explain delays or changes, and make requests in a polite and useful way. The main goal is to help you write messages that are easy for the reader to understand and answer.

`Module 3` moves into common internal communication tasks. You practise writing procedures, internal notices, problem explanations, and meeting summaries. In this module, you learn how to give clear information inside an office or team.

`Module 4` develops writing for outside communication and clearer explanation. You learn how to write external inquiries, explain situations more fully, respond to confusion, and keep email style consistent across similar messages. This module helps you choose the right tone and the right amount of detail for the reader.

`Module 5` focuses on editing and revision skills. You practise checking writing for accuracy, clarity, and tone, and you learn how to improve weak drafts step by step. This module helps you become a more independent writer who can review and improve your own work.

`Module 6` brings the earlier skills together in larger final tasks. You write related documents for different readers, keep several messages consistent, and complete a final set of revised work. The goal of this module is to help you write clearly for different readers and improve your work across a full set of workplace writing tasks.

## Section Guide

`A. Warm-Up` helps you start the unit. It introduces the topic, connects it to real work, and helps you notice the main writing problem before the lesson begins.

`B. Example Check` shows you a model or a pair of examples. This section helps you compare versions, notice important features, and see why one piece of writing works better.

`C. Language` teaches the language or writing pattern for the unit. You study useful phrases, short examples, or controlled tasks so you can prepare for the writing work that comes next.

`D. Guided Practice` gives you supported writing practice. You may use a model, a template, supplied content, or a short scenario so the writing task feels clear and manageable.

`E. Freer Practice` gives you a more independent writing task. You still use the same target skill, but with less support, so you can show that you can use it in a realistic situation.

`F. Editing Practice` helps you improve writing by checking and revising it. You may correct problems, rewrite weak parts, or review a draft more carefully.

`G. Self-Check and Reflection` helps you look back at your work. You check what you can do now, notice what still feels difficult, and think about how the skill connects to your real workplace writing.

`H. Homework & Extension` helps you transfer the unit skill to another task. It may ask you to continue the same kind of writing at home or use the skill in a more real-world situation.

## Activity Guide

`Edit` asks you to correct or polish writing carefully. You focus on fixing problems, improving accuracy, and making the text more professional.

`Example` shows you a model text or sample. You read it to see what the writing looks like before you analyse it, improve it, or write your own version.

`Learn` gives you a short teaching point to read and understand. It explains an idea, a writing principle, or a useful way to approach the task.

`Language` gives you useful words, phrases, or sentence patterns to use. It helps you build the language support you need before writing.

`Notice` asks you to look carefully at something and spot an important feature. You may compare versions, identify a problem, or notice what makes a text work.

`Rewrite` asks you to change given text into a better or more suitable version. You work from supplied content and transform it in a clearer or more useful way.

`Revise` asks you to improve a draft you already wrote. You go back to earlier work and strengthen the content, structure, or tone.

`Structure` helps you organise writing clearly. It shows parts, order, layout, or a planning frame so you can build a text in a logical way.

`Write` asks you to produce your own text. You use the target skill more independently and create a new response for the task.
